**AIM:**

This study will be aimed to explore the prospect of using multifunctional Near-Infrared (NIR) Quantum Dots (QDs) to accomplish the following:

❖    Bio-imaging by the use of NIR-QDs;

❖    Targeted drug delivery by the exploitation of both, folate receptor (FR) overexpression on the cells and Cathepsin-B (Cts-B) abnormal proteolytic activity; 

❖    Inhibition of early phase of metastatic process epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and invasion, by two methods:

➢    Inhibition of Class II Myosin motor activities by the use of (-)-para-Azidoblebbistatin;

➢    Inhibition of Cathepsin-B proteolytic activity by the use of specific mechanistic inhibitor substrates.

**OBJECTIVES:**

❖    Application of NIR QDs, made of CdSe + CdSeTe core and ZnS shell, with the following compounds attached to them for specific functions to accomplish the aforementioned aims :-

➢    **Azidoblebbistatin (AzBLE):** A drug which reversibly inhibits Class II myosin molecular motors by blocking myosin in an actin-detached state via binding with high affinity to the myosin-ADP-Pi complex;

➢    **Folic Acid (FA):** To increase the specificity of drug delivery by exploiting FR overexpression on the T-47D cells; and

➢    **Acyloxymethyl ketone (AOMK) containing specific inhibitor of Cts-B (Ac-Cha-Leu-Glu(Bzl)-Arg-AOMK):** Attached to Folic Acid molecules present on the QDs to hamper the interaction of FA with FR, which decreases the FR-mediated intake of QDs. Cts-B, overexpressed in the microenvironment, will interact with the peptide portion (designed specifically for Cts-B) of the inhibitor substrate. The AOMK group will then bind covalently to Cts-B, not only irreversibly inactivating Cts-B proteolytic functioning but also freeing FA molecules to interact with FR.       

**RESEARCH METHODOLOGY:**

❖    The required NIR QDs (Qdot™ 705 ITK™ carboxyl quantum dots), T-47D cell line, blebbistatin, FA and other general reagents are available commercially;

❖    AzBLE is synthesised using blebbistatin as a starting material through a two step method developed by Képiró _et. al._  (Azidoblebbistatin, a photoreactive myosin inhibitor. _Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America_, _109_(24), 9402–9407);

❖    The substrate inhibitor of Cts-B (Ac-Cha-Leu-Glu(Bzl)-Arg-AOMK) is synthesised by the process of fmoc-SPPS as used by Poreba _et. al._ (Fluorescent probes towards selective cathepsin B detection and visualisation in cancer cells and patient samples, _Chem. Sci._, 2019, 10, 8461-8477) but with a slight change in the procedure (use 2,4-Diamino benzoic acid instead of 2,4-Dimethyl benzoic acid);

❖    Specific functionalization of the QDs is done as follows:

➢    AzBLE is attached to the -COOH groups by carboxylic-azide reaction; 

➢    FA molecules are attached to -COOH groups using EDC/NHS coupling;

➢    AOMK containing Cts-B inhibitor is attached to FA also by EDC/NHS coupling.

❖    Characterization of QD conjugates are done by the following techniques:

➢    UV-Vis Spectroscopy;

➢    Dynamic Light Scattering;

➢    NMR (Nuclear Magnetic Resonance) Spectroscopy;

➢    Transmission Electron Microscopy.

❖    Targeted _in vitro_ imaging by QD-conjugates is done in T-47D cancer cell cultures by measurement of fluorescence at 660 nm after excitation the NIR-QDs at 585 nm;

❖    Analysis of Cts-B inhibition by QD-conjugates is done _in vitro_ by using ACC fluorescent substrates;

❖    Inhibition of the processes of EMT and invasion after treatment with QD-conjugates is analysed by:

➢    Measuring the expression of EMT-associated proteins (E-cadherin, N-cadherin, Vimentin, SNAIL);

➢    Measuring the migratory abilities of cells through _in vitro_ scratch assay;

➢    Assessing the cytotoxicity of the Qd-conjugates using the MTT assay.

❖    Control samples are used for the following purposes:

➢    QD-FA conjugates without Cts-B inhibitor bound to FA for comparing the difference in QD targeting and assessing the significance of exploiting Cts-B overactivity for targeted theranostics.

➢    Studying the effects of QD-FA-AzBLE conjugates (without Cts-B inhibitor bound to FA) on the processes of EMT and invasion.  

**SCOPE:**  

❖    The scope of this study will include the following

➢    Assessment of increased targeting theranostic capabilities of QDs by exploiting both the overexpression of FRs and the overactivity of Cts-B found in T-47D breast cancer cell line.

➢    Learning the extent of inhibition of early phases of metastasis (EMT and inhibition) that can be achieved by targeting two key components (Class II myosin motors and Cts-B) of the incredibly complex molecular pathways associated with metastasis. 

**LIMITATIONS:**

❖    The limitations of this study will include the following:

➢    The study is performed in an _in vitro_ model of breast cancer, so the significance of the results will have to be assessed further in murine models and clinical samples;

➢    In addition to Cts-B, there are numerous different proteases involved in the process of metastasis which have been found to function abnormally in the tumour microenvironment, so further studies will have to be conducted to understand the implications of Cts-B inhibition on the tumour microenvironment;

➢    The substrate inhibitor used in this study is specific for Cts-B, so while this will have a benefit of increasing targeting specificity of the QDs, there exists the possibility that once sufficient Cts-B molecules have been irreversibly inhibited, the FA-directed intake of QD-conjugates is significantly reduced.
