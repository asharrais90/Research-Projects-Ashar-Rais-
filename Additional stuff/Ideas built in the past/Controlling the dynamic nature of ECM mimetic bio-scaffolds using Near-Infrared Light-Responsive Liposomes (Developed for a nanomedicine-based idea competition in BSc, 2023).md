Development of bio-scaffolds which can mimic the properties of natural ECM is under 
intensive research. These bio-scaffolds have incredible potential to be beneﬁcial in cell 
cultures, drug-related studies and formation of tissue/organ transplants. Herein, I 
propose a method for precisely controlling the dynamical nature of ECM mimetic 
bio-scaffolds using near infrared (NIR) light-responsive liposomes (LRLs), which contain 
matrix modifying agents and signaling factors.

KEY POINTS:
- Features of the LRLs: .
	- Dipalmitoyl  phosphatidylcholine  (DPPC),  a  thermosensitive  phospholipid,  is  the main component of the membrane bilayer.
	- Brij-58, a biocompatible non-ionic surfactant, is incorporated into the membrane bilayer for the purpose of enhancing protein release through creation of stable nanopores in response to mild hyperthermia.
	- Surface coated PEGylated-Gold nanoparticles (PEGylated AuNPs) are used as plasmonic agents to bring about photothermal release of liposomal content following NIR irradiation (650-950 nm). The excitation peak of these AuNPs can be adjusted by modifying their volumes, which would allow stimulation of only the desired LRLs by using particular wavelengths.
	- LRLs are attached to the bio-scaffold by employing polydopamine (PDA) mediated layer-by-layer coating methods.
- Multiple  short  pulses  (𝛍s  range)  of  laser  light  (speciﬁc  NIR  wavelength)  are  used  to stimulate a particular type of LRLs with incredible spatial control.
