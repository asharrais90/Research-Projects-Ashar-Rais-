Biological systems are shaped not only by their molecular composition but also by their physical context. Cells continuously sense mechanical, structural, and microenvironmental cues that influence signaling, fate decisions, tissue organization, and disease progression. The projects that comprise this file originate from my interest in exploring how mechanical, structural, and microenvironmental properties regulate immune behavior, tissue organization, and disease progression, and how computational biology and nanotechnology can be used to study or manipulate these systems. This endeavor is not intended to be a showcase of completed work, but a transparent record of learning how to think as a researcher. Each project is designed to produce reusable artifacts—literature syntheses, evidence maps, computational models, visualizations, and documented workflows—that together demonstrate an evolving approach to asking and investigating scientific questions.

Intellectual Motivation

Many biological problems that appear unrelated share remarkably similar underlying principles. A thymocyte deciding whether to survive negative selection, a cancer cell escaping its primary tumor, an axon growing out of a neuron, a stem cell committing to a lineage and an endothelial cell responding to blood flow all operate within mechanical microenvironments that shape signaling, behavior, and fate. This observation motivates the central idea behind this portfolio:

Different biological systems often solve different problems using similar physical and mechanistic principles.

Understanding how physical context shapes biological function is not just an academic question. It has direct implications for understanding autoimmune disease (where thymic selection fails), cancer progression (where the tumor microenvironment drives metastasis), and tissue regeneration (where mechanical cues guide vascular and stem cell behavior). This portfolio is an attempt to build a unified framework for thinking about these problems—one that connects fundamental biology to computational modeling to potential therapeutic interventions.

Research Philosophy

My interests are driven less by individual diseases than by recurring mechanisms. I am particularly drawn to questions that sit at the intersection of:

  • The physical context of cells (mechanobiology, mechanotransduction, tissue architecture, ECM organization).
  • The cellular decisions that arise from that context (immune-cell development, cell-cell and cell-stroma interactions).
  • The tools to study and manipulate these processes (systems-level computational modeling, nanotechnology).

Portfolio

Project 1: Thymic Selection Mechanobiology

This project is built upon the central question of how the mechanical properties of thymic stroma (such as stiffness, fiber orientation and viscoelasticity) influence thymic selection process. It is composed of two sub-projects: 1. A scoping review done to map the evidence present in the literature regarding this topic. Scoping Review- Modulation of thymic selection outcomes by stromal or substrate mechanical properties (SMP). 2. A hybrid simulation designed to explore different mechanistic avenues that allow the stromal mechanical properties to influence the selection process. The work for this sub-project is presented in the following notes: - N1: Overview, rules and outputs for the hybrid model on the influence of thymic stromal stiffness upon thymic selection outcomes. - N2: Calibration, sensitivity analyses and validation of this thymic selection mechanobiology model. - N3: Parameters and calculations for the thymic mechanobiology model Key outputs from this project include: - Evidence map for the literature on the relationship between stromal mechanical properties and selection outcomes; - Suggestions regarding possible future work that can adequately fill the gaps currently present in the literature to progress the field; - Hypotheses associated to the effects on changing thymic stromal stiffness upon the selection outcome fractions generated by the hybrid model; - Sensitivity analyses which give a sense of the main factors in the link between the stromal stiffness and selection outcomes; Ideas for future work on thymic mechanobiology

Guiding Principle

While the biological systems differ, the underlying questions remain consistent:

  • How does structure influence function?
  • How do cells interpret their physical environment?
  • How do mechanical cues interact with molecular signaling?
  • Can computational models help reveal mechanisms that are difficult to observe experimentally?
  • How might these insights inform future diagnostics, therapeutics, or biomaterial design?

This portfolio is an ongoing attempt to explore these questions through the integration of literature synthesis, computational biology, and scientific visualization.
