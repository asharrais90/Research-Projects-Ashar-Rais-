
A pre-metastatic niche (PMN) is a specialized, supportive microenvironment created in distant organs by a primary tumor _before_ cancer cells arrive that acts as a receptive "soil" for future metastasis. Primary tumors have been observed to send out systemic warning signals that prepare secondary sites:
- **Tumor-Derived Secreted Factors (TDSFs):** Soluble proteins and growth factors (such as VEGF, TGF-β, EGF, and IL-6) released into the bloodstream
- **Extracellular Vesicles (EVs) and Exosomes:** Tiny membranous packages carrying microRNAs, proteins, and lipids that fuse with cells in distant organs to reprogram them.
- **Bone Marrow-Derived Cells (BMDCs):** Hematopoietic progenitor cells (such as VEGFR1-positive cells) mobilized from the bone marrow to home in on future metastatic sites.
